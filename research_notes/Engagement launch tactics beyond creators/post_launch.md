# Post-launch growth of survivors-likes, bullet heavens and small roguelites (2021-2026)

Labels used throughout: **[Dev]** = developer/publisher-reported; **[Tracker]** = SteamDB/third-party tracker data (observed CCU, review counts); **[Est]** = modeled estimate (Boxleiter, Gamalytic, Sensor Tower, GameSensor, etc.); **[Opinion]** = analyst/author interpretation.

## Q1. How did Vampire Survivors, Brotato, Halls of Torment, DRG: Survivor, Soulstone Survivors, Death Must Die, Megabonk, Ball x Pit, Terminus, Dome Keeper and similar games grow after launch, and what triggered spikes?

### Takeaway
Two patterns show up. Some games had near-zero launches and grew through creator discovery, weekly patches and a low price (Vampire Survivors, Terminus). Others had strong launches and kept growing through creator cascades in the first weeks (Megabonk peaked 17 days after launch), then 1.0 launches, console ports and free or paid content drops. The biggest documented CCU spikes match streamer pickup and content patches landing on the same days, not discounts.

### Cited Findings

**Genre peak-CCU benchmarks (SteamDB, compiled by HTMAG, April 2024) [Tracker]**
- Peak CCU: Vampire Survivors 77,061; Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor 56,943; Brotato 32,216; Halls of Torment 26,061; Soulstone Survivors 18,854; Death Must Die 13,131; 20 Minutes Till Dawn 12,953 — [HTMAG, DRG Survivor spinoff](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/04/08/how-creating-a-spinoff-for-a-hot-genre-lead-to-success-for-deep-rock-galactic-survivor/)
- Megabonk later topped all of these: 117,336 CCU on 5 Oct 2025 [Tracker] — [SteamDB via Steam News](https://store.steampowered.com/news/group/4777282/view/538866028337168955)

**Vampire Survivors (poncle): the main "tiny launch, then breakout" case**
- First build was on itch.io on 31 Mar 2021, about 9 months before Steam. The Steam page was "coming soon" for only about 2 weeks (Valve's minimum). It launched in Early Access on 17 Dec 2021 at $3 with about 90 wishlists. The developer had about 1,000 Twitter followers and did not tweet on launch day. It had fewer than 10 reviews in its first month — [HTMAG, Jan 2022](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/01/31/vampire-survivors-success-an-opportunity-in-the-steam-marketplace/)
- The developer patched on 24 Dec 2021 despite having fewer than 10 reviews, and "patched at least weekly after launch" — [HTMAG, Jan 2022](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/01/31/vampire-survivors-success-an-opportunity-in-the-steam-marketplace/)
- Trigger: SplatterCatGaming found it about 3 weeks after 6 Jan 2022. His friend Northernlion then played it on stream several times, and the follower chart rose sharply — [HTMAG, Jan 2022](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/01/31/vampire-survivors-success-an-opportunity-in-the-steam-marketplace/). Wikipedia says YouTube creator attention in early 2022 lifted Steam sales — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_Survivors)
- About $1.5M gross in just over a month, at $3 with a 30x Boxleiter multiplier [Est]. The price was not raised after streamers picked it up — [HTMAG, Jan 2022](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/01/31/vampire-survivors-success-an-opportunity-in-the-steam-marketplace/)
- The all-time CCU record of 77,061 was set on 20 Feb 2022, about 2 months into EA. It was about 6,000 above the previous week's record and coincided with patch 0.2.13 (a new character). A new stage was due later that week, and patch 0.3.0 (new mechanic and stage) was announced for the week after [Tracker] — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/vampire-survivors/player-count-new-stage)
- The developer quit his job in Feb 2022 and hired freelancers in Mar 2022. 1.0 came out on 20 Oct 2022 [Dev] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_Survivors)
- By July 2022 players were declining. The genre-wide October 2022 rebound is credited to VS 1.0 plus Brotato and Soulstone Survivors; players "just needed new content." The first major DLC came in Dec 2022 [Opinion/Tracker] — [HTMAG, "Are Vampire Survivor-likes dead?", Jan 2023](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/01/09/are-vampire-survivor-likes-dead/)
- Steam Deck's release likely boosted sales [Opinion] — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/how-vampire-survivors-went-from-hobby-project-to-game-of-the-year/1100-6511980/) (page returned 403 to fetch; claim taken from search snippet)
- "27M+ players since 2022, per poncle, April 2026" [Dev, via secondary] — [Icon Era](https://icon-era.com/statistics/vampire-survivors), attributed to an interview with The Game Business that I did not verify. Steam estimate: about 6.5M copies and about $26M gross [Est] — [Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/1794680/Vampire_Survivors)

**Terminus: Zombie Survivors (Longplay Studios / In-geon): slow EA, breakout at 1.0**
- The Steam page went up 15 Feb 2021 (only the 2-week minimum). EA launched 28 Feb 2021 in **Korean only**, with 1 of 6 endings and about 100 wishlists. It had 12-15 reviews in its first month and took almost a year to reach 100 reviews [Tracker/Dev] — [HTMAG, Sept 2024](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/09/09/success-after-a-slow-ea-launch-the-terminus-story/)
- Trigger 1: the English translation in Aug 2021 (it had 32 reviews at that point). The first major English YouTuber video followed on 3 Aug 2021. HTMAG says it "didn't take off until English was added and English streamers began playing" — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/09/09/success-after-a-slow-ea-launch-the-terminus-story/)
- Trigger 2: repeat creator coverage. Splattercat covered it four times during EA: 185K views (Aug 2022), 250K (Mar 2023), 88K (Nov 2023) and 109K (Aug 2024). An Aug 2022 Fanatical bundle "did not visibly change the follower curve" — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/09/09/success-after-a-slow-ea-launch-the-terminus-story/)
- About 3 years of content updates (e.g., v0.9.8 added 15 occupations and a boat escape ending) kept it visible. It had about 60,000 wishlists a month before 1.0 — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/09/09/success-after-a-slow-ea-launch-the-terminus-story/)
- The 1.0 push on 19 Aug 2024 sponsored only streamers who had already played Terminus or Project Zomboid. It also used a Keymailer masthead, paid ads and press releases. The game was featured in New & Trending and got 800 reviews in its first 10 days. By 30 Aug 2024 it had 2,241 reviews and 2,130 CCU — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/09/09/success-after-a-slow-ea-launch-the-terminus-story/)
- 100,000 copies sold about a month after 1.0 [Dev] — [Games Press](https://www.gamespress.com/Terminus-Zombie-Survivors-Surpasses-100000-Copies-Sold)
- HTMAG's counterfactual: launching with an English translation, at least 7,000 wishlists and a Next Fest slot would likely have produced faster growth [Opinion] — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/09/09/success-after-a-slow-ea-launch-the-terminus-story/)

Vedinad withdrew from TGA 2025 Best Debut Indie because he had shipped games before under other names — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabonk)

### Inferences
- Spikes cluster around three things: (1) creator discovery, especially a big streamer setting off copycat coverage (VS/Splattercat then Northernlion; Megabonk/xQc then shroud then others); (2) a content patch landing while that attention is high (VS's 77K record matched a new-character patch); and (3) milestone launches such as 1.0, a console port or a co-op mode.
- "Peak after launch day" is the signature of a creator-driven breakout. Megabonk's CCU rose about 5.6x from launch day to day 17. VS peaked about 2 months into EA.
- Sales milestones are almost always announced alongside new content (Ball x Pit at 1M and 2M, Halls of Torment at 1M, CloverPit's Hard Mode). Milestone plus update is a standard PR beat that gives press a reason to cover a game again.
- Cheap prices (VS $3, Halls of Torment $6.66, Megabonk $9.99, Ball x Pit $14.99) are a shared trait of the genre's breakouts.

### Gaps
- No SteamDB CCU time series was retrieved for update days on Brotato, Halls of Torment, Ball x Pit or Megabonk, so update-specific lift is not quantified for them.
- No developer postmortem was found for Megabonk, Ball x Pit or Death Must Die.
- A search summary claimed Vampire Survivors' Steam monthly average rose about 44% in Nov 2025 (peak about 15.5K) after its 2025 co-op update. I could not confirm which tracker published this, so it is unverified.
- Brotato's EA-period cadence and its CCU per update were not found.

## Q2. What update cadence do successful small roguelites use in the first 1-3 months, and do updates measurably bring back players and creators?

### Takeaway
The documented hits patched very fast: VS weekly from day 7, and DRG:S shipped a new biome at week 5. After the first 3 months they settled into roughly quarterly major drops, often free. Ball x Pit shipped three free updates over 8 months; Terminus and Halls of Torment ran multi-year EA update programs. The best hard evidence that updates bring players back is VS's record CCU on a patch week, plus the genre-wide October 2022 rebound when new content arrived. Systematic measurement is scarce.

### Cited Findings
- VS: first patch on 24 Dec 2021 (day 7, with fewer than 10 reviews), then "patched at least weekly after launch." HTMAG names "frequent patches and responsiveness to the community" as a breakout factor — [HTMAG, Jan 2022](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/01/31/vampire-survivors-success-an-opportunity-in-the-steam-marketplace/)
- VS: the 77,061 CCU record (20 Feb 2022) came with patch 0.2.13 (new character), about 6K above the prior week's record. A new stage followed that week and patch 0.3.0 the next — roughly one content beat a week [Tracker] — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/vampire-survivors/player-count-new-stage)
- Genre: players declined by July 2022 and recovered in October 2022 with VS 1.0 plus new entrants. HTMAG concludes players "just needed new content to play" [Opinion] — [HTMAG, Jan 2023](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/01/09/are-vampire-survivor-likes-dead/)
- DRG:S: EA launched 14 Feb 2024 and the first content update (Salt Pits biome) came 20 Mar 2024, about 5 weeks in — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Rock_Galactic:_Survivor). PC Gamer quotes the developers on players' "ravenous appetite" for content — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/deep-rock-galactic-survivor-sails-past-a-million-sold-as-developers-salute-players-ravenous-appetite-for-space-dwarf-content/)
- Ball x Pit: launched 15 Oct 2025; three free updates were announced at 1M (Dec 2025). They shipped Jan 2026 (Regal), Apr 2026 and 6 Aug 2026 (Naturalist, the final one), roughly every 3-4 months. Sales went from 1M (Dec 2025) to 2M (Aug 2026) during that update run [Dev] — [Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/2025/12/ball-x-pit-sales-top-one-million-three-free-content-updates-set-for-2026); [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2026/08/devolvers-brilliant-roguelite-dopamine-factory-ball-x-pit-is-a-smash-hit-at-2-million-sales). The coverage does not attribute sales to the updates.
- Terminus: about 3 years of EA content updates. Splattercat came back to cover it four times (Aug 2022 to Aug 2024, 88K-250K views each), suggesting updates gave creators reasons to return [Tracker/Opinion] — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/09/09/success-after-a-slow-ea-launch-the-terminus-story/)
- Halls of Torment: about 16 months of EA (May 2023 to Sep 2024) before 1.0, with "more free updates planned" after 1M — [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/10/halls-of-torment-hits-1-million-sales-more-free-updates-planned/)

**Free updates vs DLC**
- Free-update model: Ball x Pit (three free 2026 updates) — [Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/2025/12/ball-x-pit-sales-top-one-million-three-free-content-updates-set-for-2026)
- Hybrid model: Halls of Torment paired paid DLC "The Boglands" with the free "Bardcore" update and its console launch — [Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/2025/09/halls-of-torment-for-ps5-and-xbox-series-launches-october-28-alongside-dlc-the-boglands-and-free-update). Brotato paired paid DLC (Abyssal Terrors) with free local co-op and console ports — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brotato). Dome Keeper launched paid DLC about 3.5 years after release — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1637320/Dome_Keeper/)
- Cosmetic supporter DLC at launch: DRG:S's $4.99 pack had a 20% attach rate falling to 16%, versus 8% or less typically [Est] — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/04/08/how-creating-a-spinoff-for-a-hot-genre-lead-to-success-for-deep-rock-galactic-survivor/)

**Early Access vs 1.0**
- 1.0 can be the bigger moment for small games. Halls of Torment went from 905K to 1M within about 3 weeks of 1.0 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halls_of_Torment); [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/10/halls-of-torment-hits-1-million-sales-more-free-updates-planned/). Terminus got 800 reviews in its first 10 days at 1.0 after about 100 reviews in its first EA year — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/09/09/success-after-a-slow-ea-launch-the-terminus-story/)
- Big EA hits show the reverse. GameDiscoverCo found Hades II hit an all-time 112K CCU at 1.0, but month-1 revenue at 1.0 was down 75% versus its EA launch — [GameDiscoverCo, EA graduates 2025](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-early-access-graduates)
- Soulstone Survivors combined 1.0 with day-one PS5/Xbox (17 Jun 2025) — [Rogueliker](https://rogueliker.com/soulstone-survivors-console-announcement/)

### Inferences
- A workable cadence from these cases: hotfixes and small patches weekly in month 1 (VS), a first real content update at weeks 4-6 (DRG:S), then major free updates every 2-4 months (Ball x Pit), with milestone PR tied to each.
- Updates seem to matter most when they land while creator attention is already high (VS patch-week records) or when they give a returning creator new material (Terminus/Splattercat).
- For a game that sold modestly in EA, 1.0 acts like a second launch: new Steam visibility, press coverage and a sponsored-creator push. Games that sold huge in EA should expect 1.0 to sell well below the EA launch.

### Gaps
- I found no systematic dataset (e.g., from GameDiscoverCo) measuring CCU or sales lift from free content updates in this genre. The October 2026 GameDiscoverCo "CCU staying power" piece does not cover post-launch updates — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/steam-games-with-ccu-staying-power)
- No data on whether creator coverage (video counts or views) measurably returns after updates, beyond the Terminus/Splattercat anecdote.

## Q3. What features make a game more likely to be streamed or clipped (visual escalation, build-breaking, short runs)?

### Takeaway
The best-documented factors are short runs (VS rounds last 1-15 minutes), a low impulse-buy price that lets a streamer say "buy it now," deep unlock and meta-progression with secrets, and polished game feel. The rest — escalating screen-filling chaos, broken builds, audience participation — is credited informally. Evidence on Twitch integrations, daily seeds, leaderboards, speedrunning and modding is thin in the sources I found.

### Cited Findings
- HTMAG's breakout explanation for VS: a $3 price let streamers say "buy it now" as an impulse buy rather than a wishlist. Other factors: streamer enthusiasm creating urgency, polished, bug-free game feel with strong pacing, frequent patches, and short, deep runs (about 1-15 minutes) with deep meta-progression and many achievements. HTMAG argues a $10-25 mid-priced game lacks that hype [Opinion] — [HTMAG, Jan 2022](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/01/31/vampire-survivors-success-an-opportunity-in-the-steam-marketplace/)
- Hidden characters create the "one more run" feeling [Opinion] — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/features/vampire-survivors-is-the-top-game-on-steam-heres-why)
- DRG:S design lessons: simple controls (movement only, no dash) with a deep meta-game, leaning "hardcore" (deep, not merely hard). A streamer video about how hard the game is drew attention [Opinion] — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/04/08/how-creating-a-spinoff-for-a-hot-genre-lead-to-success-for-deep-rock-galactic-survivor/)
- Megabonk: a big-streamer cascade (xQc, shroud, then many others); over 2M Twitch hours and a 58K peak concurrent viewers — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/what-is-megabonk-viral-vampire-survivors-clone-3256658/). Twitch viewing was still sustained 9-10 months later (149K hours in June 2026) — [Streams Charts](https://streamscharts.com/games/megabonk)
- Terminus: one streamer played 8h19m and returned several times. Paid 1.0 sponsorships went only to streamers who already played Terminus or Project Zomboid, i.e., genre-matched creators — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/09/09/success-after-a-slow-ea-launch-the-terminus-story/)
- Audience participation: one commenter says VS's stream integration lets viewers pick items during broadcasts, and that big streamers play it between other content. This is an unverified comment, and I did not confirm that VS has such a feature — [Bottomfeeder Substack comments](https://bottomfeeder.substack.com/p/the-fast-life-and-fiery-death-of/comments)
- Weekly challenges: Halls of Torment added a weekly Challenge Shrine (no engagement data) — [Steam news hub](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2218750/allnews/)
- Streamer-friendly genres beyond roguelites: GameDiscoverCo calls Mimic Party (about 300K sold) "streamer-friendly," and notes a trend of short roguelike/incremental runs being added to slower genres (Combolands: above 50% of its 2,000+ peak CCU retained, about 100K sold) — [GameDiscoverCo, Oct 2026](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/steam-games-with-ccu-staying-power)

### Inferences
- From these cases, the clip-friendly recipe is: a run that resolves within one stream segment; a mid-run power fantasy where the screen fills with effects and the build goes off; secrets and unlocks chat can speculate about; and a price low enough that viewers buy during the stream.
- Genre-matched creators who already like adjacent games (Project Zomboid for Terminus; roguelite variety streamers like Splattercat and Northernlion for VS) are more valuable than reach alone.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced, quantified evidence on Twitch-integration features (chat voting, viewer item picks), daily or weekly seeded runs and leaderboards, speedrun communities, or modding (e.g., Brotato's modding scene) as growth drivers in this genre. These need targeted research, e.g., speedrun.com category counts, Steam Workshop subscriber counts, and patch notes adding Twitch integration.
- No clip-platform (TikTok/YouTube Shorts) data was found for any of these games.

## Q4. What first Winter Sale discount do small indies use in their launch year, and with what results?

### Takeaway
Recent breakouts used shallow first Winter Sale discounts. Megabonk took 25% off about 3 months post-launch, Ball x Pit 20% off about 2 months post-launch, and Balatro 15% off in its launch-year Winter Sale. Terminus held full price for about 10 months, then used the minimum 20% that triggers Steam wishlist emails. I found no public data on what those Winter Sale discounts produced.

### Cited Findings
- Winter Sale 2025 (18 Dec 2025 to 5 Jan 2026): Megabonk was **25% off ($7.49 from $9.99)**, about 3 months after its 18 Sep launch — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/round-up/2025/12/18/the-best-steam-winter-sale-discounts-on-2025-games); [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/147268/weekend-pc-download-deals-for-dec-19-steam-winter-sale-2025-and-friends). Beebom listed $7.99 (20%) instead, so sources conflict — [Beebom](https://beebom.com/best-steam-winter-sale-2025-game-deals/)
- Winter Sale 2025: Ball x Pit was **20% off ($11.99 from $14.99)**, about 2 months after its 15 Oct launch — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/round-up/2025/12/18/the-best-steam-winter-sale-discounts-on-2025-games); [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/147268/weekend-pc-download-deals-for-dec-19-steam-winter-sale-2025-and-friends)
- Kotaku: 2025-released indies were mostly 15-38% off, e.g., Peak 38% off (to $5) and Urban Myth Dissolution Center 15%. The prior year's standout, Balatro (Feb 2024 launch), was only **15% off** in the 2024 Winter Sale — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/steam-sale-2025-best-indie-games-2000654842)
- Terminus: no discount for about the first 10 months, then "the minimum 20% discount that triggers Steam email notifications" to wishlisters, later a June 2024 Daily Deal — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/09/09/success-after-a-slow-ea-launch-the-terminus-story/)
- VS: kept at $3 through the breakout, with the price unchanged after streamers picked it up — [HTMAG, Jan 2022](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/01/31/vampire-survivors-success-an-opportunity-in-the-steam-marketplace/)
- Vendor guidance (unverified numbers): 20-25% for the first major sale 1-3 months after launch; if you launched at 10% off, step to 20-25% — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-discount-strategy). Launch at full price, then small, gradual discount increases, avoiding deep early cuts — [Trapplan](https://www.trapplan.com/en/blog/steam-discounts-and-sales-strategy-for-indie-games-in-2026)
- Steam launch discounts are capped at 40% and run 7-14 days (per a Steam-related source in search results; not verified against Valve docs) — [Steam sales calendar](https://steamdb.com/en/tools/steam-sales)

### Inferences
- The norm among recent hits is 15-25% at the first Winter Sale, keeping deep discounts in reserve. That protects perceived value while still triggering wishlist emails (20% minimum) and sale-page visibility.
- Already-cheap games ($3-$15) have less room to discount and depend on price-as-impulse rather than discount-as-event.

### Gaps
- No public Winter Sale results (units or revenue lift) were found for Megabonk, Ball x Pit, Balatro or other launch-year survivors-likes. GameDiscoverCo Winter Sale 2025 analysis was not found in search results; it may be paywalled (GDCo Pro).
- First-3-month discount data for VS, Brotato, Halls of Torment and Soulstone was not retrieved. SteamDB price history would answer this.

## Q5. Examples of games that launched to small numbers (hundreds of sales) and broke out later, and why

### Takeaway
The two best-documented cases are Vampire Survivors and Terminus. VS launched with about 90 wishlists and fewer than 10 reviews in month one, then about $1.5M in roughly 5 weeks after Splattercat and Northernlion found it. Terminus launched with about 100 wishlists, Korean-only, and took about a year to reach 100 reviews, then reached 100K sold at 1.0. In both, the breakout came from creator discovery after the game was playable and polished, with constant updates keeping it alive until then. Terminus also needed localization to reach English creators, then a properly marketed 1.0.

### Cited Findings
- VS: about 90 wishlists, a 2-week coming-soon page, a $3 price, and fewer than 10 reviews in month one. Breakout 4-6 weeks after launch via SplatterCatGaming then Northernlion, about $1.5M estimated gross in about a month after that, and 77K CCU by week 9 — [HTMAG, Jan 2022](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/01/31/vampire-survivors-success-an-opportunity-in-the-steam-marketplace/); [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/vampire-survivors/player-count-new-stage)
- Terminus: about 100 wishlists and 12-15 first-month reviews. The English translation came 6 months in, there were 4 Splattercat videos over 2 years, it had about 60K wishlists before 1.0, and 100K copies sold within about 1 month of 1.0 (3.5 years after EA launch) [Dev/Tracker] — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/09/09/success-after-a-slow-ea-launch-the-terminus-story/); [Games Press](https://www.gamespress.com/Terminus-Zombie-Survivors-Surpasses-100000-Copies-Sold)
- HTMAG warns that relying on a viral breakout is an "all-or-nothing" strategy [Opinion] — [HTMAG, Jan 2022](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/01/31/vampire-survivors-success-an-opportunity-in-the-steam-marketplace/)
- Base rates: one vendor estimate puts the median indie at about 200-400 copies. Top-tier games benefit from algorithmic amplification, word of mouth and streamer coverage [Est, modeled] — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/indie-game-sales-statistics)
- Megabonk is a milder "grew after launch" case: about 21K CCU at launch rising to 117K by day 17 through streamers — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/megabonk-roguelike-steam-concurrent-player-records-daily-growth/); [SteamDB via Steam News](https://store.steampowered.com/news/group/4777282/view/538866028337168955)
- Nomad Survival (solo dev) shows the modest middle: about $221K gross in 6 months — [HTMAG, Jan 2023](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/01/09/are-vampire-survivor-likes-dead/)

### Inferences
- Shared mechanics of slow-then-breakout: (1) the game is very cheap or very good value; (2) the developer ships constantly, so whenever a creator finds it there is fresh content and visible support; (3) one mid-size genre creator's coverage leads to peers covering it too; (4) localization or 1.0 removes a barrier and resets visibility.
- Weak or no pre-launch marketing did not doom these games, but HTMAG's view is that Terminus would have grown faster with standard pre-launch steps (7K+ wishlists, Next Fest, English at launch).

### Gaps
- I found few other well-sourced 2021-2026 survivors-like or roguelite cases with launch sales in the hundreds and a later breakout. A targeted search of HTMAG and GameDiscoverCo "slow burn" case studies, or of individual dev postmortems on Game Developer, would add examples.
- No first-month unit sales are public for VS or Terminus; their launch size is inferred from wishlists and review counts.
